About this visual

What you are looking at. Every plan of one kind over 3 or 4 items, as a grid: the plan's notation, the 16 components of its output, and a coloured frame shared by plans that give the same output vector.

How to read it. Choose A, B, C or D, the reading, the number of items and the item set. A frame colour that repeats means those plans agree. Click a cell for its output components and its firing sequence.

What it shows. How many different vectors each kind makes. For generic sedenions in the element reading every plan is different (3 items: 2, 6, 8, 12 plans, all distinct; 4 items: 5, 24, 40, 120). In the operator reading the outputs follow the firing sequence only, so A gives one output and C gives as many as it reaches orders (22 for four items). The item sets show where plans collapse: one sedenion repeated (power-associative: the brackets stop mattering) and real scalars (everything commutes and associates).

What it does not show. Which plan is right: the page does not rank plans. Plans of one kind overlap those of another (A, B and C are all inside D), so the counts are plan counts, not counts of distinct vectors over the union.

Numbers to check. Counts of plans: A 2, 5; B 6, 24; C 8, 40; D 12, 120 (n = 3, 4); fire sequences reached with n = 4: 1, 24, 22, 24.

Code and data. Everything shown is recomputed in the browser by bf.js (the sedenion product, the plans, the four orderings, both readings, exact counts with BigInt, set membership) and compared with the numbers the Python engine produced (bf_data.js); the result is printed above. Light and dark follow the system.
