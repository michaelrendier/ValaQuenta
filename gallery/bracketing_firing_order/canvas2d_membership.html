Set membership: has it been here before?

The third tool: a memory of the positions visited, the flip test, and the jurisdiction check, for any sequence of functions. A picture shows a structure; it is not evidence that the structure means anything beyond its definition.

Jurisdiction: is the operation in the object's own set?
About this visual

What you are looking at. Set membership, the third tool of the engine: a memory of where a sequence has been. Any sequence of functions can be run from a starting number; the page records the positions visited, finds the returns (collisions), and answers, before a step is taken, whether it would land on a visited position.

How to read it. Pick Recamán's sequence (the flip between subtracting and adding is exactly this membership test), a preset word, or build your own from the buttons. Under the picture are the collisions and the verdicts of the flip test for candidate next steps. The second panel is the jurisdiction check: a map from each object to the operations legal for it, and a request tested against it.

What it shows. That Recamán's rule is a set-membership test against the whole history, not a threshold (the first terms 0, 1, 3, 6, 2, 7, 13, 20, 12, 21, 11, 22 come out of the engine's would_collide); that Recamán's sequence still revisits some numbers, because only the down-step is tested; that a word can return to a position it left (+2 then −2); and that a violation of a jurisdiction is a failed membership test, nothing more.

What it does not show. Anything about why Recamán's sequence behaves as it does (that is open), or any claim that the jurisdictions in the default map are the right ones: the map is supplied by the caller and the page only tests membership.

Numbers to check. Recamán, 40 terms, equal to the engine's (OEIS A005132); +2, −2 from 1: collision (0, 2) at 1; lineage with build_up: violation, emerger with build_up: legal.
