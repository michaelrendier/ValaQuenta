How many plans: the counts to sixteen items

A, B, C and D against n on a log scale, with Bell(n) and 2ⁿ⁻¹ for comparison; exact values below. A picture shows a structure; it is not evidence that the structure means anything beyond its definition.

About this visual

What you are looking at. How many plans there are, for n items from 1 to 16, on a log scale: A (Catalan(n−1)), B (n!), C (Catalan(n−1)·2ⁿ⁻¹), D (n!·Catalan(n−1)), and for comparison Bell(n) (set partitions, tool 1) and 2ⁿ⁻¹ (contiguous blocks, which is what the Emerger's {4:8:4} is).

How to read it. Read the height as the number of digits. The table below gives every value exactly (computed with BigInt in the browser).

What it shows. D is the largest kind and contains A, B and C: D = (2n−2)!/(n−1)!, every ordered binary product of the n items. At n = 16: Bell 10,480,142,147; contiguous blocks 32,768; A 9,694,845; D 202,843,204,931,727,360,000. Three different meanings of bracketing give three different counts, so a claim should say which.

What it does not show. Which plans matter. A count is not a measure of importance, and these are plan counts, not counts of distinct outputs.

Numbers to check. n = 3: A 2, B 6, C 8, D 12; n = 16: Bell(16) = 10,480,142,147; 2¹⁵ = 32,768.
