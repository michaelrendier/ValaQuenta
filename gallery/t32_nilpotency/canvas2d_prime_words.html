Primes as T32 words: the Hyperwebster address and the nilpotency split

Each prime p is written in decimal, read as a bijective base-97 number over a QWERTY-row character table (the Hyperwebster address), and cut to 32 bits: its T32 word. The word is nilpotent or it is not. A picture shows a structure; it is not evidence that the structure means anything beyond its definition.

About this visual

What you are looking at. A bridge from integers to the algebra. A prime p is written in decimal; its digits are read as digits of a bijective base-97 number over a 97-character QWERTY-row table (the Hyperwebster address); the address is cut to its low 32 bits, which is read as a word of T32/GF(2). Each row shows p, its address, the word as 32 bars, and whether the word is nilpotent.

How to read it. Bars are bits of the word, high bit on the left, coloured by the class of the word (blue nilpotent, orange not) when the bit is 1 and pale when 0. ◆ marks the five Niemeier-gap primes (11, 17, 31, 47, 59). Click a row to outline its decimal digits in the character table below; the digit characters 1…9, 0 have indices 1…10. The summary gives the nilpotent count, the chance calculation at your baseline, and the control from the Python engine.

What it shows. Counts: 11 of 16 sedenion basis primes and 12 of 15 Moonshine primes are nilpotent, and all five gap primes are. The honest comparison is the control: a random word is nilpotent 50% of the time (the default baseline here, not the 70% an earlier wiki page used), and five of five has probability 1 in 32 against random words but about 1 in 8 against random primes of the same size (0.127, from matched_prime_control). The excess lives in the two-digit range; from three digits up primes and composites are both 50%.

What it does not show. That nilpotency marks primes: over 2…100,000 the Hyperwebster words of primes are 49.1% nilpotent and composites 50.0%. The factoring use of the same mechanism failed its control (Ainulindale wiki 77). This page is a count and a control, not a signal. The 16 sedenion basis primes are every prime up to 53, so their own control is vacuous.

Numbers to check. sedenion basis (16): 11 nilpotent = 68.8%. Moonshine (15): 12 = 80%. Gap primes: 5 of 5; prime-matched P(5 of 5) = 0.127. Addresses are exact (BigInt) and match the Python engine on 12 test values.
