The exact grid: who commutes, what closes

Eighteen elements in exact rational arithmetic and the 324 ordered products of a window onto Aff(1, ℝ). A picture shows a structure; it is not evidence that the structure means anything beyond its definition.

About this visual

What you are looking at. An exact finite window onto the group: 18 elements with add ∈ {−2, 0, 3/2}, scale ∈ {1/2, 1, 3}, sign ∈ {−1, +1}, in exact rational arithmetic. Row i, column j is the product xi∘xj. The page is a map of three facts about those 324 products.

How to read it. Choose what the colour shows. commute: a cell is filled when xi∘xj = xj∘xi. closure: filled when the product is again one of the 18 elements. add: the offset of the product on a diverging scale. Click a cell for the exact product both ways.

What it shows. That the group is associative (all 5832 triples are checked on load: zero failures) but far from commutative (76 of the 324 ordered pairs commute) and that the window is not a subgroup (only 84 of the 324 products stay inside). The centre of the window is the identity alone.

What it does not show. A Cayley table of a finite group. A finite group's table is closed, and this window is not (84 of 324 products stay inside); the only finite subgroups of Aff(1, ℝ) have order 1 or 2. The picture is a census of a sample.

Numbers to check. 5832 triples, 0 associativity failures; 76 commuting ordered pairs; 84 products inside the grid; centre = {identity}.
